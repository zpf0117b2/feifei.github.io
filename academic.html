PhD Student, University of California, Irvine

I am a PhD student at UC Irvine working on multimodal large language models for human-centric multimedia understanding, spanning video and speech, and on low-resource settings where training corpora are limited. My work centres on adapting large language models to low-resource domains in human-centric tasks: human-centric visual understanding, action tokenization, audio and speech recognition and reasoning, and co-speech visual alignment and generation. I am also involved in projects on multimodal LLMs for low-resource languages and cultures, and on personalized agents for healthcare systems.

Research

Multimodal LLMs for low-resource speech and visual understanding and generation

Bridging human-centric video, audio (speech) and text into a unified semantic space. By action tokenizing and temporally synchronizing across modalities, such as aligning speech with co-speech gestures or visual actions, LLMs can generate coherent, context-aware outputs that reflect human-like perception and behaviour. This enables gesture generation, motion synthesis and audiovisual narration grounded in natural language.

LLM-enhanced recognition and reasoning on low-resource audio

Integrating large language models with traditional ASR pipelines to improve transcription accuracy, particularly in domain-specific and noisy scenarios. By leveraging external knowledge sources such as medical knowledge graphs, together with contextual understanding, LLMs can correct recognition errors, disambiguate terms and enhance spoken question answering.

Personalized agents for healthcare systems

Autonomous agents that analyse individual patient data, such as medical history, lifestyle and preferences, to deliver tailored health advice and treatment options. These systems interact dynamically with users and with other agents, adapting recommendations in real time to improve decision-making and patient outcomes.
